Why it lands on the spot

The longer you stand still, the sharper it gets.

A phone never reports one true position. Every fix arrives with a radius of uncertainty that shrinks as the receiver locks on to more satellites.

While you are saving a pin, Loadstone keeps reading and averages the fixes by how much each one can be trusted — tighter readings count more, wild ones are thrown out, and walking away starts it over. You watch the number fall and save when you are happy with it.

The figure it shows is honest. Averaging cannot beat the hardware, so Loadstone never claims to be more certain than the readings actually support.

Three steps, start to finish

How it works

STEP 1

Drop a pin

Stand where you want to remember. Wait a few seconds while the accuracy tightens, then save.

STEP 2

Name it

Add a name, a note, a category, and photos of the surroundings so the place is easy to recognise.

STEP 3

Follow it back

One arrow points to the pin and the distance counts down. Hold the phone flat and walk.

What you get

Built for the last hundred meters

A true compass arrow

Corrected to true north with your local magnetic declination, so it works standing still.

Photos of the place

Snap the surroundings when you save a pin. They stay on your phone, and never travel in a shared link.

Share a spot

Send a pin as a link through any app. It opens straight in Loadstone, ready to save.

Works without signal

No tiles to download and no network needed. GPS and the compass are enough.

Material You

Light and dark themes that follow your system and pick up your wallpaper colours.

Metric or imperial

Set from your region automatically, with a switch in settings when you want the other one.

Free and open source

Nothing to pay for, nothing hidden.

Loadstone is free software. The whole app is on GitHub — read it, build it yourself, file an issue, or send a patch. There is no analytics library, no advertising SDK, and no server that your pins are sent to.

Your pins, notes and photos live on your phone. A pin only leaves your device when you choose to share one, and a shared link carries the name, note and coordinates — never a photo.
